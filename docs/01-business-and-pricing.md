# 01 · Business Model, Packages and Pricing

## 1. Positioning

> **"See every shilling before you spend it."**

Hornbill Smart Security Services publishes the full bill of materials — every camera, every metre of cable, every connector, every hour of labour — for a complete security installation, with prices, before you call anyone.

This works because of a verified gap. Kenyan competitors divide into two camps and neither crosses over:

| Camp | Example | Publishes | Never publishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shops | Shopit, Hubtech, Alltech, Glantix | Exact SKUs with exact prices | What a finished installation costs |
| Installers | ORACO, CCTV Solutions, Lance, AreaSpy | Package totals, sometimes contents | What each line costs |

ORACO gets closest — an 8-camera package at KES 72,000 with contents listed — but no unit prices. CCTV Solutions lists every component of a 4-camera kit at KES 36,000, again with no unit prices. **A page showing "4 × DS-2CD1043G2-LIUF/SL @ 11,900 = 47,600" alongside cable metres, accessories, labour and a total does not exist in Kenya today.**

Two demand signals that this is wanted, not merely absent — these are real Kenyan Google autocomplete strings: `electric fence quotation pdf in kenya`, `electric fence materials price list`, `cctv installation cost calculator`. People are searching for the exact artefact nobody publishes.

**Proof the mechanism converts locally:** Premier Automatic Gates runs a working configurator with an itemised Description/Spec/Qty/Amount output and a PDF export — for gate automation only. Someone will port it to CCTV. Move first.

### The second, larger advantage: the coast

Nairobi is contested. AreaSpy holds 28 Nairobi area pages; Lance publishes monthly; Hubtech lists 576 SKUs. **The Kenyan coast is empty.** Jiji's entire "CCTV installation services, Mombasa" category returns four listings. No competitor has a purpose-built Nyali, Bamburi, Shanzu, Mtwapa, Diani, Ukunda, Kilifi or Malindi page. AreaSpy — the strongest location-page operator in the market — has zero coast pages.

Hornbill is Mombasa-based. **Launch coast-first.** It is the difference between fighting for position 8 in Nairobi and owning position 1 in Nyali within weeks.

## 2. Service lines

All of the following are **confirmed in scope** by the owner. The core lines ship first; the additions follow in Sprints 6–8 but are designed into the data model and navigation from day one.

**Core:** CCTV — residential and commercial · Electric fencing · Razor wire and perimeter · Gate automation · Video intercom · Access control, biometrics and time attendance · Smoke and fire detection · Computer networking and structured cabling · Cable management and containment · Two-way radio and POC communications · Fuel monitoring · Server and control-room build-out · Smart home and nanny cameras.

**Recurring revenue — confirmed, and the biggest gap in the current model:**
- **Annual maintenance contracts.** Flat annual fee per system, parts excluded — the Kenyan convention. Published range for small residential systems is 5,000–15,000/yr; a 32-camera commercial site with quarterly PPM visits is far higher. Price in tiers by camera count.
- **Cloud and offsite recording.** 500–1,500 per camera per month by resolution and retention. The only true per-camera-per-month recurring line with published Kenyan rates.
- **Remote monitoring.** 3,000–10,000/month, scaling with camera count and response level.
- **Scheduled system health checks.** Sell as part of the AMC, not separately.

**Compliance services — confirmed. Both are legally mandatory, recurring, and no competitor sells them:**
- **Fire safety compliance.** Under OSHA 2007 and the Fire Risk Reduction Rules 2007, every Kenyan business needs a county Fire Safety Certificate (prerequisite for the Single Business Permit; Mombasa 2,500–8,000) and a **mandatory annual fire safety audit** by a DOSH-registered auditor. Non-compliance: fines to KES 500,000 and/or six months.
- **CCTV data-protection compliance.** The ODPC Draft Guidance Note for Private Security (Dec 2025) requires visible surveillance signage, a stated lawful basis, a justified retention period, DPIAs for high-risk processing, and subject access to footage **within 7 days**. Sell it as a package: signage, retention configuration, DPIA support.

**Confirmed additions:**
- **Power backup as a productised line.** UPS, batteries, inverters, small solar. Coastal outages kill CCTV and nobody sells the fix as a product — attach it to every install.
- **GPS vehicle tracking and fleet telematics.** Pairs directly with fuel monitoring and shares the buyer. The owner already works as a telematics consultant.
- **CCTV installer training courses.** `cctv installation course in kenya` and `cctv courses in mombasa` are both verified low-difficulty queries. Revenue, authority and backlinks in one product.

**Entrance control and screening — new category, added after reviewing keensight.co.ke:**
- **Boom barriers and traffic barriers** — estates, hotels, office parks, the port corridor.
- **Parking ticket dispensers and parking management** — ticketing, RFID, barrier integration. Real Mombasa demand: malls, hospitals, hotels.
- **Baggage and luggage X-ray scanners** — hotels, malls, the port, government buildings. High ticket, low competition, and the coast has more scanner demand per capita than anywhere in Kenya because of tourism and the port.
- **Walk-through metal detectors and handheld scanners** — schools, events, hotels, banks.
- **Turnstiles and full-height gates** — already adjacent to the access-control line.
- **Road blockers and bollards** — high-security commercial.

These distinguish the site from every generic Kenyan CCTV shop and give the catalogue the range the owner wants. Seed with placeholder prices, editable from admin.

## 3. The three-layer catalogue

**Items** — every SKU. Own page, specs table, use cases, price, "works with" links. Browsable, searchable, quotable. Never individually checked out.

**Solutions** — a complete installation as a bill of materials over Items plus labour. Lines are split:
- **Primary items** — what the client thinks they're buying (cameras, recorder, energizer, gate motor).
- **Secondary items** — what the job actually needs and clients forget (cable, HDD, PSU, adapter boxes, baluns, connectors, junction boxes, trunking, clips, brackets, earth rod, testing).
- **Labour and services** — installation, configuration, commissioning, handover training.

**Services** — priced per point, per metre, per door, per day.

Every Solution line shows quantity, unit price and extended price, and totals to one number. That visible arithmetic *is* the product.

## 5. The Solution Builder

Six questions → a live, editable, itemised BOM.

1. Property type — home / apartment / shop / office / warehouse / school / estate / farm or site
2. How many cameras, and how many are outdoors
3. Analog or IP (with a "help me choose" that recommends and explains)
4. Colour at night? (drives ColorVu vs EXIR)
5. How many days of footage to keep (drives HDD sizing)
6. Location — Mombasa / Nairobi / coast / upcountry (drives travel and cable assumptions)

Output: the full BOM with quantities, unit prices, extended prices, subtotal, VAT line, total. Every line swappable. Then: **Request Quote** → saved quote at `/q/{CODE}` → branded PDF → WhatsApp with the code pre-filled → lead in admin.

Recommend the technically correct system, and show a cheaper alternative beside it rather than silently downgrading.

## 6. Quantity rules — how we handle "it depends"

The owner's position is correct: cable runs, trunking and labour genuinely vary by site. **So we do not hardcode them.** Every quantity is a formula over named constants stored in a `pricing_rules` table and editable from the admin portal. The site labels all builder output as an **indicative estimate, confirmed at site survey** — which is honest, and which is exactly what makes publishing it safe.

Ship with these defaults. They are industry-typical starting points, not claims about any particular job. Tune them in admin once against a few real past quotes and they become genuinely accurate.

## 7. Pricing rules

- **Public price = distributor cost × 1.40**, rounded to nearest 100, **VAT-exclusive**, KES.
- **`cost_price` is never exposed to the browser.** RLS plus explicit select lists.
- **Market-ceiling override.** Consumer smart-home and solar SKUs carry a `market_ceiling_price`; effective price is `min(cost × 1.4, ceiling)`. Already applied in the seed: EZVIZ `CS-HB8c/SP` computes to 18,200 but Jumia sells it at 17,499, so it is capped at **16,500**. Margin there comes from installation.
- **Never mark up a reseller price.** Techyshop, Hubtech, CCTV Shop Kenya, Protech Line and Jumia figures already carry 25–105% over dealer. Observed spreads on identical SKUs: Nemtek Druid 15 LCD 20,900 dealer vs 43,000 reseller; ZKTeco MB360 24,900 vs 45,000.
- **Some seeded rows are sell prices, not cost prices.** Where `price_basis = 'owner_sell_price'` the figure is already public and **no markup is applied** — it is stored as `price_override`. This currently covers **fuel monitoring only** (KES 45,000 installed per vehicle). Surveillance hard disks are owner-confirmed distributor cost and take the normal ×1.4: 1TB 6,500→9,100 · 2TB 12,500→17,500 · 4TB 24,000→33,600 · 8TB 45,000→63,000. **Watch the 4TB and 8TB against market** — published Kenyan surveillance-drive listings run lower, so set a `market_ceiling_price` on those two once you have compared.
- **Everything is editable from admin.** Placeholder and mock prices are seeded as ordinary rows so the owner can correct them himself. No price is ever a constant in code.
- Prices reviewed **monthly**; the review updates the `prices_updated_at` site setting, which drives the visible stamp and every `dateModified`. The owner publishes a blog post with each review — see `docs/10-content-calendar.md`.
- **Deposit** 50% before installation. M-Pesa Paybill **222111**, Account **3033552**.
- **Site survey** mandatory, **KES 1,000** commitment fee, deducted from the final invoice. Owner-confirmed framing, which the page must carry verbatim in substance: **a written findings report, camera positions marked up, and the fee credited to your invoice.** Never present it as a bare fee — the Kenyan norm is a free survey, and nine competitors offer one.

## 8. Prices that must be verified before they go live

The catalogue seed is trustworthy — those are your own distributor's figures. These are not, and are flagged in the CSV:

- **Fuel monitoring** — resolved. Owner sets **KES 45,000 installed per vehicle**, which sits between the two firm public Kenyan anchors (40,000 and 55,000). Component-level pricing stays unpublished; quote static-tank and generator monitoring individually.
- **POC / network radios and repeaters** — Kenyan stockists publish nothing above handset tier. Handsets are solid; repeaters and base stations are quote-only.
- **Per-data-point cabling rate** — no Kenyan integrator publishes one. Validate against two Nairobi contractors.
- **VMS licensing, precision cooling, environment monitors** — no Kenyan public pricing exists.
- Two catalogue SKUs need a model number confirmed with the supplier (motorized varifocal 4MP/6MP), and four intercom kits plus one NVR need prices.

**One firm figure worth using in sales copy:** a CA radio frequency licence costs KES 19,700 (1,000 application + 18,700 licence) and takes 74–106 days. Licence-free PMR handsets need none. Telling a client that upfront is the kind of specific, useful fact that wins the job.
